Testing the Activity console

The database holds three months of made-up but realistic trading, built so this screen has something to say. Here’s how to get in, what to try, and what has changed since you last read this.

Getting in

npm run dev open http://localhost:3000/api/dev-login?email=admin@kavenue.fr

That signs you in as the admin and drops you on the console. On the live site it’s admin.kavenue.fr, same account, no shortcut needed.

Six things to try

1

The front page

Three sentences today. Each one names the Driver or the trip it’s about.

Is it too much, or not enough? It stays silent when a check finds nothing — no “0 problems”, no green ticks.

2

The trip that was dropped three times

Trips → find Cannes → Valberg, 31 July. It sat in the Pool nine days and three Drivers took it and gave it back.

Can you follow the story? Every step in order, with times.

3

Ask why a Driver can’t take a trip

On any trip, type a name into the find box or click one. On a live trip it tells you what stopped them; on a finished one, who could have taken it.

This is the one you asked for first. Does the answer land in one read?

4

The trip nobody can serve

The front page names it: Valberg → Marseille Airport. It needs a First-class van and the only one is 60,4 km away on a 60 km limit.

Is four hundred metres a good enough explanation? It used to say “60 km on a 60 km limit”, which read like a broken rule — that was rounding, and it’s fixed.

5

Search someone

Type Nadia, or Negresco, or a town.

Does it find what you meant? It searches names, hotels, references and routes — nothing else yet.

6

A hotel’s record

Hotels → Belles-Rives. Smallest account, worst luck: 10 of its 42 trips found nobody — and the page no longer says so at the top, it says it on each of the ten rows.

Would you call them about this? If not, the screen isn’t doing its job.

The six I said were wrong — all fixed

A hotel’s own name is off its own rows.

Belles-Rives now reads “→ Nice Airport, T2”. An arrow pointing back (“← Nice Airport, T1”) means the car was bringing someone to the hotel. Matched on the map coordinates, not the name.

The roll-up counts are out of both headings.

Every row already says Unfilled or Completed for itself.

The Driver picker has a find box.

Type three letters instead of reading thirteen names. It works the same at fifty.

Lists are grouped — by day on Trips, by month on a hotel’s or a Driver’s own page.

By day everywhere was tried first and looked wrong immediately: a hotel books about one trip a day, so it made forty headings over forty rows.

Lists say what they are not showing.

“Newest 40 of 110”, with a link to the older ones. They used to just stop.

The Drivers list says who is working.

“83 trips · last 25 Aug”, or “never taken a trip” in amber. Three of the thirteen have never taken one.

And the one you spotted

The Drivers live in towns now, not in hotels.

Nice, Cannes, Antibes, Mougins, Monaco. It was only ever the test data — the app has always asked a Driver for their own address. Each town was picked close enough to the old one that none of the three months of history stopped making sense, and the one deliberate gap in the fleet — nobody who can reach Valberg — is still there.

One thing to know before you trust a number

Everything before today is made up, and the screen says so. Each entry in a trip’s story is tagged test data. Only the handful of trips posted today are real records. That tag is deliberate — without it, three months of invented history would look exactly like the real thing, to you and to the app.

And one real problem is open. On cancellations, the price the box quotes and the price we charge can disagree. It only became visible with this new data, it isn’t something this week broke, and it’s first in the queue next time.
